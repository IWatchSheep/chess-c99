OWNER'S ORIGINAL INTENT
Two chess decision strategies use the same legal rules and starting positions.
Side A — FORGETS / DIVES: prioritizes direct offensive opportunities (the queen-and-bishop Scholar's Mate attack is a teaching example), under-considering defensive relations and responses.
Side B — REMEMBERS / COORDINATES: considers the *entire team's* legal moves, who protects whom, effective width (coordinated useful movements, numerical pressure and protection), enemy responses, and the value/order of exchanges. A pawn-for-queen exchange may be advantageous. Defense does not mean refusing to attack or sacrifice.
Hypothesis to TEST, not assume: preserving and using relational information can yield better decisions than a direct-opportunity strategy, under some conditions. Also identify counterexamples.
