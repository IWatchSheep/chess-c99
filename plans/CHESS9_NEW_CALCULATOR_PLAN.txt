CHESS(9) — NEW CALCULATOR PLAN (OWNER-LED RESET)
Date: 2026-10-24
Status: PLAN ONLY. No Step 3 calculator has been added to chess(9).c.

0. BASELINE AND CHANGE CONTROL
- Begin from the owner's original chess(9).c, not chess(10).c.
- The base is C99 and retains standard legal moves, selection, preview of a proposed destination, BEFORE/AFTER lists for all other pieces on both sides, GAINED/LOST/UNCHANGED destination comparisons, and confirmation before finalizing a move.
- Treat the earlier Gemini Side A/Side B scoring, 'safe width' sums, and mate-threat heuristics as historical experiments, NOT a required implementation.
- Keep chess(9).c intact. Make any future experiment in a new version after verifying the design and running regression tests.

1. OWNER'S INTENDED WORKFLOW (ALREADY PRESENT AT THE BASELINE)
A. Select a piece: show the selected piece and squares legally available to it.
B. Select one of those squares: do not move permanently; preview what the move would do to the squares available to EVERY OTHER friendly and opposing piece.
C. Read the results as a machine-readable text list as well as on a human-friendly board.
D. Confirm the move before changing the real game position.
E. The chess rules remain authoritative. Preview must not commit a move.

2. OWNER'S NEW CALCULATOR QUESTION
Given a proposed move from square X to square Y, can a calculator identify follow-up moves that bring two or more friendly pieces alongside, into mutual support, or into protective alignment on a route toward one or more specified goal squares? Can it compare this coordinated or 'width-wise' route with an apparently shorter direct route by an individual long-moving piece? Can it account for the enemy's available defensive blocks and the value of pieces potentially exchanged?

The owner's distinctive observation concerns how many pieces CAN'T MOVE to useful squares after each possible move. An enemy move might disable multiple follow-up destinations or block a queen-and-bishop route. The calculator should examine these restrictions for defense and again with roles reversed for offense.

3. DEFINITIONS TO AGREE ON BEFORE ANY FORMULA OR CODE
Do not collapse these into one metric:
- Legal destinations: where a piece may legally move now, including empty-square moves and captures.
- Geometric control/defense: squares a piece attacks or protects, including friendly-occupied squares; differs from legal destinations.
- 'Can't move' must be split into explicit observations:
  (a) a piece has zero legal moves in the current position;
  (b) a particular destination available BEFORE is LOST AFTER;
  (c) a square or route is inaccessible due to a blocker or king-safety rule;
  (d) a move exists but is strategically undesirable (NOT the same as illegal).
- Protection: piece P geometrically defends the occupied square of friendly piece Q. Record which named pieces are involved, not just totals.
- Width (owner's provisional term): several pieces cooperating via follow-up motion, protection, numerical support, or beneficial exchange possibilities; NOT mere adjacency, not movement distance, and not just count of legal moves.
- Goal: an externally chosen square or set of squares; initially a test parameter, NOT always the enemy king. Test first goal G1 and then a second goal G2.
- Piece exchange value: approximate conventional piece values may be logged, but chess legality and checkmate must remain separate. A low-for-high trade may be worthwhile even with a sacrificed friendly piece.

4. CALCULATOR INPUT / OUTPUT CONTRACT (DESIGN PROPOSAL, NOT IMPLEMENTATION)
Inputs: original board B, candidate legal move m, goal set G (possibly G1 then G2).
First compute B' = hypothetical result of m, without committing.
Output, per piece ID (color, type, starting square), and for both sides:
- legal destination sets BEFORE and AFTER;
- GAINED / LOST / UNCHANGED destinations;
- explicit zero-legal-move pieces BEFORE and AFTER;
- protective relationships BEFORE and AFTER, listing every formed/broken link;
- enemy blocking/countermove candidates and their effects on those lists;
- prospective follow-up friendly moves that establish protection or convergence toward G;
- potential exchange sequence and net material change, with uncertainty indicated where deeper replies are not checked.
The report must distinguish directly calculated facts from estimates or speculative plans.

5. NON-DUPLICATED COUNTING OF 'CAN'T MOVE'
First identify the UNIT OF COUNTING before calculating:
- Unique piece that currently has zero legal moves (count each piece once).
- Unique (piece, destination) relationship LOST after a proposed move (count each pair once).
- Unique destination square denied across the team (count each square once even if multiple pieces lose it).
These are THREE DIFFERENT counts. Never sum or cancel them as though equivalent.
For sequential hypothetical moves, recompute the state and calculate the difference between consecutive states; do not repeatedly charge the same disabled piece or lost (piece, square) relationship as a NEW loss.
For each possible enemy response r, compute B'' = Apply(B', r), show which lost relationships are restored and which new ones are lost, then reverse colors and repeat for offensive opportunities.

6. FIRST TEST SCENARIOS (DO NOT BIAS THE ANSWER)
Test A: Empty or nearly empty small legal position to establish accurate before/after and counting behavior.
Test B: A friendly piece protected by another friendly piece, then a legal move that breaks that protection; differentiate protection from legal mobility.
Test C: One enemy blocking move restricting multiple legal destinations, and one reply restoring a subset; verify no double counting.
Test D: Scholar's Mate attack: after 1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nc6 3.Qh5, check Black's 3...g6 and the queen/bishop f7 route. This is a test of enemy defensive blocking, not proof that a particular approach always wins.
Test E: A legally constructed favorable low-value-for-high-value exchange with at least one opposing reply.
Test F: Same initial position, two different externally specified goals G1 and G2; verify route/planning output changes in a traceable way.

7. IMPLEMENT IN SMALL STAGES ONLY AFTER AGREEMENT
Stage 3A — Independent, pure before/after relation extractor, with no scoring or move selection. Test each set exactly.
Stage 3B — Nonduplicated mobility-loss counts and defensive-blocking report, for one candidate move and each legal enemy reply. Show examples with actual square names.
Stage 3C — One- and two-step protective follow-up descriptions toward goal(s); explicitly label any assumption about opponent cooperation.
Stage 3D — Legal exchange-sequence analysis (including capture order, promotions, pins and checking legality as appropriate); clearly bounded by search depth.
Stage 3E — Only after the above pass, compare candidate routes and optionally experiment with different agent decision policies. Do not bake in which strategy should win.

8. QUALITY GATES
- C99 only, beginner-readable functions and comments.
- Preserve chess(9) legal-move rules and selection/preview/finalize behavior.
- Strict compile: gcc -std=c99 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Werror.
- Add deterministic assertions for every example, including opponent replies and cases where a concept is NOT applicable.
- Verify that preview leaves real board, history and turn unchanged.
- Distinguish same-piece identity across movement, and handle captures/promotions correctly.
- Avoid treating the same future square as multiple unique disabled squares merely because many pieces refer to it.
- Show concrete before/after examples and exact calculations before proposing numerical weights.
- No changes to chess(9).c before owner approves a specific implementation step.
