2. Claude's reading
a) One combined list for the side to move, two groups, captures first
   (this answers your question: the owner's option is "captures first"):
   - Group 1, captures ("good trades"): the capture-value chain (idea 5),
     greatest captured value = IF, each smaller value ELSE IF, smallest
     ELSE; between captures of the same value, fewer squares first
     ("capture being true you move in the least amount of squares to
     greatest").
   - Group 2, all other moves: the distance chain (idea 4), fewest
     squares = IF ... most = ELSE.
   Both numbers (value, squares) stay printed on every line; the group is
   named on every line, so the two IF chains cannot be confused.
b) "Transition ... then go back to the first list": the list is rebuilt
   in every position. While a good trade exists, group 1 heads the list;
   when none remains ("you can't keep capturing good trades"), only the
   distance chain is left - back to the first list; when a capture
   reappears later, group 1 is on top again. In the loop (3C) and trace,
   each position would state which list heads it (CAPTURE or DISTANCE).
c) This is an ORDER of listed facts. It does not choose, play or score a
   move, and is not called "best".

3. The open point: what is a "good trade"? (asked to the owner too)
   (a) any capture (idea 6: captures of pieces worth more are "good");
   (b) a capture where the value taken is at least what can be lost: e.g.
       the captured value >= the capturing piece's value, or the capture
       square is not attacked by the other side (no recapture possible).
       Captures failing this would go to group 2 (by distance), marked
       CAPTURE-NOT-GOOD, so nothing is hidden.
   Claude proposes to offer (b) as a stated, testable condition (raw
   values P1 N3 B3 R5 Q9 logged, no weighted score), but only if the
   owner chooses it; otherwise (a).

Reading: a trade is not judged by one capture. For each capture c of S,
the exchange tree that follows on the board is listed over several plies
(N, the user's limit, as in 3C): every legal sequence of captures and
recaptures, each side allowed to stop, and at the end of each sequence the
running material totals of both sides (MATERIAL White <sum> Black <sum>,
P1 N3 B3 R5 Q9, logged only) = "a summation over multiple moves". Then, in
the 3C / 3D-2 form, without a single score:
  GOOD-TRADE FORCED      S's total gain minus its total loss is >= 0 at the
                         end of every line the other side can choose (S may
                         stop capturing at any point);
  GOOD-TRADE POSSIBLE    >= 0 in some lines but not forced;
  GOOD-TRADE ELSE        < 0 in every line within the limit.
  LIMIT N when the tree was cut by the limit (it is a bounded summation).
So a low-value capture can be a good trade (FORCED) and a high-value
capture a bad one (ELSE), as idea 8 says. Group 1 of the combined order
(section 2) = captures that are GOOD-TRADE FORCED or POSSIBLE (labelled),
ordered by the value taken (idea 5), ties by fewer squares; group 2 = all
other moves by distance, captures that are GOOD-TRADE ELSE marked as such.
Whether group 1 should hold only FORCED, or FORCED and POSSIBLE, is a
choice for the owner (Claude proposes both, labelled).
"Over multiple loops of the different pieces": the tree includes captures
on other squares too? Claude proposes, for 3E, captures on the same square
(the exchange), with "exchange <square> N" for one square and the
per-capture GOOD-TRADE label computed on the capture's square; captures
elsewhere (in-between moves) belong to the 3C loop.
