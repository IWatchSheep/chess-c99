How Claude proposes it works (PLAN.md step 3, SPEC Section 5b, stage 3C)
- Snapshot = one move, then every reply (stages 3A and 3B).
- Loop of depth N = the full tree of every legal sequence of N plies from the
  position, nothing skipped. At every node the same measurements as the
  snapshot, with the charge-once rule (a loss is charged at the ply where it
  happens; still missing later = not charged again; back again = restored).
- It reports, without choosing a move: every sequence ending in checkmate;
  for a first move, whether an outcome is forced (against every reply) or
  only possible ("k of r replies"); sequences leaving the other side with at
  least k zero-move pieces.
- Evidence that it finds strategies it was not told about: from the start
  position, 0 mates within 3 plies, exactly 8 at 4 plies (all Fool's Mate:
  f3/f4/g4, ...e5/e6, the other pawn, ...Qh4#) and 347 at 5 plies - the
  published counts. Cost grows ~30x per ply (4 plies < 1 s, 5 plies ~20 s on
  Claude's machine), so 3C has no pruning and the depth is the user's choice;
  pruning is a separate later decision because it changes what can be found.

The owner's method (his words in PLAN.md Appendix A; PLAN "The method",
SPEC Section 5c, stage 3S)
- Steps 1-4 (select, list, choose, before/after lists of all other pieces)
  already exist. The calculator is an imaginary loop of steps 1-4 counting how
  the lists' numbers change, repeated until Scholar's Mate is built, so that
  a math logic can be read from it and tried on other strategies.
- Start with fewer pieces, then build up. This needs FEN position setup first
  (new stage 3S, with legality checks).
- Evidence: K+Q vs K forced mate in 2 found from 50 positions; from the full
  board after 1.e4 e5, 5 plies = 23,027,889 positions (~2 min). The build-up
  ladder (White Ke1 Qd1 Bc4 vs Black Ke8 Pf7, Black pieces added one by one,
  Qh5 then Qxf7 with Black passing): the black king's legal squares after
  Qxf7 are 1, 1, 0, 0 for Pf7 / +Pd7 / +Qd8 / +Bf8 - mate appears when
  Black's own Qd8 blocks the last escape; Bf8 is not needed. With only
  Ke8 + Pf7, no forced mate within 3 White moves (34,537 positions).
- Claude's caution, written into the plan: a pattern read from Scholar's
  Mate must be checked on other mates and on non-mating lines.

Other content of the specification (unchanged from 1.0 except stage letters)
- L (legal destinations) and A (controlled squares, incl. friendly-occupied,
  ignoring pins) kept separate; protection links never point at a king.
- Your three counting units as U1 (zero-move pieces), U2 (lost (piece,
  square) pairs) and U3 (squares denied to the team), never added.
- Routes compared as a vector (own moves, protectors, attackers, material at
  risk), no weights; "coming up protectively" = a support follow-up.
- Stages: 3S position setup, 3A facts report, 3B replies, 3C loop, 3D goals,
  3E exchanges,
  3F comparison / Side A-B experiment.
- Scholar's example: 3.Qh5 pins f7 (Black loses f7-f6, f7-f5, h7-h5),
  threatens exactly one mate (Qxf7#); 28 replies, 8 stop every mate-in-one
  (Qe7, Qf6, Qg5, Qh4, Ke7, Nh6, d5, g6), 20 leave one.
