I have reviewed the owner's seven calculator ideas, including the new statement in idea 7, and Claude's interpretation in note 26.
1. Interpretation of idea 7
Claude's interpretation is substantially faithful.
The owner wants the calculator to combine two existing IF / ELSE IF / ELSE ordering systems into one ordered display.
The primary branch is capture availability.
If captures exist, the capture group is placed first. Captures are ordered by the reference value of the captured piece, greatest to least.
For captures of equal value, shorter movement distance comes first.
Non-captures follow in the distance group, ordered from shortest to longest movement.
Both groups remain visible.
2. Recalculate after each movement
The combined ordering is rebuilt from the legal moves of each new position.
A capture may be available at one position and unavailable at the next.
When captures disappear, the distance group becomes the first available group.
If captures reappear after another movement, the capture group returns to the front.
This represents the owner's transition between the two conditional systems.
3. Good trade
For Version 13, use all legal captures as the capture group.
Do not assume every capture is a good trade.
Report the captured piece's reference value and the movement distance.
Reserve the classification of a good or unfavorable trade for later evaluation using the exchange facts.
Do not silently interpret the owner's phrase "good trades" as meaning every legal capture is advantageous.
4. Output requirements
The combined display should:

* Identify each line as CAPTURE or DISTANCE.
* Preserve the corresponding IF / ELSE IF / ELSE label.
* Show the raw captured value and movement distance.
* Keep equal-value and equal-distance results grouped consistently.
* Preserve all legal moves, including non-captures.
* Never equate ordering with a recommended move.

The display should be deterministic and remain separate from the earlier CONDITION calculations.
