## 2. Answers to Claude's four questions

Question 2 — Is numerical ordering allowed without weighted scoring?

Yes. Ordering by distance is a measurement, not automatically a strategic judgment.

A one-square movement should appear before a four-square movement, but the calculator should not claim the shorter movement is better.

The protectors, attackers, goal reachability, and other measurements should remain visible independently.

Question 3 — Is NONE appropriate for unreachable goals, and what about limit 1?

Yes. For the route-length view, `NONE` can mean that the particular goal was not reached within the specified depth.

With a depth limit of one, a successful one-move route should receive `IF 1`.

However, for your new squares-traveled view, `NONE` should not be used for a legal move merely because it has the largest distance. The largest distance receives `ELSE` whenever there are at least two distinct distance levels.

Question 4 — Replace the earlier IF/ELSE or put the new ordering beside it?

Put the new ordering beside it.

This is important because your earlier IF/ELSE expresses the result of a condition, while your newest IF/ELSE IF/ELSE represents the position of a movement in an ordered distance list.

Using the same labels without distinguishing their purposes could produce confusing results.

I suggest explicitly naming the columns `CONDITION` and `DISTANCE ORDER`.

## 3. An important clarification about equal distances

Suppose a bishop can move to two different destinations that are both three squares away.

Both movements should receive the same distance level.

For example:

| Move   | Distance | Label   |
| ------ | -------- | ------- |
| `c4b5` | 1        | IF      |
| `c4d5` | 1        | IF      |
| `c4e6` | 2        | ELSE IF |
| `c4f7` | 3        | ELSE    |

No movement should receive a higher or lower priority simply because it happened to appear first in the original list.

For a piece with just one distinct available distance, I recommend `IF`, with no `ELSE IF` or `ELSE`. A piece with no legal movements has no distance order at all.

This makes the results deterministic and testable.
