## Experiment E-A — seeing versus entering (row 1)

Run with `tools/experiments/e_a_seeing_vs_entering.c` (existing chess.c functions only: `piece_attacks`, `legal_destination_map`, `load_fen`); full output in `tools/experiments/e_a_output.txt`.

- **Statement tested against** (Draft 12 §[2], owner-accepted): "Seeing the rock is a dimensional perspective and remains strictly separate from removing the rock."
- **Positions:** E5 (protection is not mobility), E3 after 3.Qh5 (the f7 pawn pinned), the start, a pinned knight, a castling position; 14 pieces.
- **Observed:** *A* − *L* was non-empty for 12 pieces and *L* − *A* for 4, with three kinds of reason for *A* − *L*: OWN-PIECE (a protected square cannot be entered: the rook a1 and b1 in E5, the queen d1 at the start), PAWN-DIAGONAL-EMPTY (a pawn watches diagonally, moves straight: the pinned f7 pawn watches e6, g6, and can move nowhere), KING-SAFETY (the pinned knight on e2 watches six squares and may enter none); and two kinds for *L* − *A*: PAWN-FORWARD and CASTLING.
- **Result: SUPPORTS** the narrow mapping that the chess program measures what a piece watches and where it may go separately, and that the two differ in both directions.
- **Limit:** this does not show that the rock is a chess obstacle, or that seeing and removing the rock are modelled.
